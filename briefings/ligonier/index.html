A Diagnostic Brief

Where the spreadsheets end, the questions begin: a candid look at operational visibility at Ligonier Ministries.

A modest proposal — that a fifty-five-year-old ministry stewarding fifty million dollars annually deserves the same operational clarity it brings to its theology.

Prepared for Ligonier Ministries leadership
Posture Diagnostic — not proposal
01 — THESIS

The most expensive number in any growing ministry is the one nobody can see.

For more than five decades, Ligonier has built one of the most theologically serious teaching ministries in the English-speaking world — a publishing house, a broadcaster, a college, a conference circuit, an internet radio network, and a global translation engine, all under one roof. The fruit is visible everywhere: a hundred million people reached each year, a Reformation Study Bible in pew and home, faithful teaching streaming twenty-four hours a day.

Beneath that fruit, however, lies an operational reality common to every fast-growing ministry of this scale: the systems that built the work are no longer the systems that can scale it cleanly. Spreadsheets, email threads, and tribal knowledge — heroic stewards of decades past — quietly become the bottleneck that obscures the answers leadership most needs to make.

There is a particular kind of operational reality common to mature ministries at this scale: organizations that collect enormous amounts of sales and engagement data across hundreds of distinct offerings — and find it difficult, or impossible, to determine the marginal profitability of any single one of them.

This brief is an attempt to take that reality seriously — to look at the public surface of Ligonier's operations, name where the visibility gaps almost certainly live, and propose a single, narrow first step that could repay itself many times over.

It is not a sales pitch. It contains no pricing. It assumes that any partner worth working with should first earn the right to be in the room.

03 — REVENUE PICTURE

Where the dollars come from — and what they hide.

The audited FY2025 statements tell a clear top-line story: roughly seventy-eight percent of Ligonier's operating cash arrives as donor contributions, with the balance flowing through three commercial-style streams — media sales, tuition and conferences, and other revenue. Each stream behaves like a different business.

FY2025 Operating Cash Receipts, by Stream
Source: Ligonier audited consolidated statements of cash flows, year ended June 30, 2025.

What the top line does not show is the operational complexity behind each stream. Contributions are eight figures across an unknown distribution of recurring versus one-time donors, restricted versus unrestricted, regional and digital and print — each with different acquisition costs and lifetime values. Media sales aggregate thousands of distinct SKUs across formats, languages, bindings, and bundles. Tuition and conferences blend a residential college, a national event, regional events, and study tours, each with its own venue economics and registration funnel.

This is not a complaint. This is what a flourishing fifty-year-old ministry looks like. It is also, almost by definition, a ministry where three or four of the most important questions leadership could ask are answered today by stitching together spreadsheets, exports, and somebody's institutional memory.

04 — THE CATALOG REALITY

One teaching series. Eight to twelve SKUs. Hundreds of series.

The diagnostic question — many offerings, opaque marginal profitability — becomes concrete the moment one walks through the public store. A single representative teaching series typically expands into several distinct stock-keeping units, each with its own production cost, royalty share, fulfillment economics, and price-volume curve.

The honest question, then, is not whether Ligonier knows what it sold last quarter. It is whether anyone in the building can answer, in under a day, which fifty SKUs subsidize the other thousand — and whether that subsidy is intentional, or accidental.

07 — RECOMMENDED STARTING POINT

The single, narrow first step.

A six-week diagnostic build that answers the central question — which of our offerings actually carry the others — using only data Ligonier already owns, delivered as a dashboard the finance team can open every Monday morning.

PHASE 01 · WEEK 1

Listen & Map

Two days on-site or remote. We sit beside the people who actually maintain the spreadsheets — finance, fulfillment, the store team, the donor team. We map every workbook, every export, every "Friday afternoon ritual." We deliver a one-page operational map by end of week.

PHASE 02 · WEEKS 2–4

Build the Spine

We connect existing systems — Shopify, accounting, royalty trackers, donor platform — into a single read-only data model. No replacements. No migrations. No risk to anything currently running. The team's spreadsheets keep working while we work behind them.

PHASE 03 · WEEKS 5–6

Deliver the Dashboard

Per-SKU margin, rolled up to series, author, category, and channel. Identifies the offerings that quietly subsidize the catalog and the ones that quietly drain it. Delivered with documentation, training, and a thirty-day support tail.

The result is not a software product. It is an answer Ligonier leadership can use immediately — to inform pricing, bundling, future series investment, and stewardship of donor dollars. If, after seeing the dashboard, the team chooses to walk away, they keep it. The work is theirs.

08 — METHODOLOGY

How we work, in plain terms.

Carolina Redesign operates on a three-phase model designed for organizations that cannot tolerate operational disruption — which is to say, every organization that has been doing meaningful work for fifty years.

PHASE 01

The Audit

A structured listening exercise. We shadow the team — not the org chart — and deliver a written scope of work. Fixed scope, fixed timeline, fixed price. No build begins until the audit is read and approved.

PHASE 02

The Build

Custom development against the approved scope. We integrate with what exists; we replace only what truly needs replacing. Weekly demos. Documented handoff. Staff training included.

PHASE 03

The Retainer

Optional, never required. A monthly retainer for ongoing tweaks, new features, and the occasional Tuesday-morning emergency. Think of it as a fractional CTO, not a vendor relationship.

We sell outcomes, not hours. Every engagement is scoped to a deliverable the client can describe in a single sentence — and every engagement ends with that deliverable in the client's hands, regardless of whether they keep us on retainer.
