How the model works, and what it can't tell you

The fish

Cod biology uses a standard surplus-production model: logistic growth7 with an optional depensation term, so a depleted stock can be made to rebuild slowly. In "Replay history" the model is driven by the recorded catch from the NAFO STATLANT 21A database1.

How well it fits

The growth parameters are fitted to one consistent series: DFO's estimate of total biomass (ages 2 and older) from the NCAM 2021 assessment2. That series starts in 19832 and ends in 20212. DFO re-estimates the whole series in every assessment, so the model names the assessment it uses. The fit was run twice, once with catch as the only extra source of deaths and once with environmental mortality added. The model on screen uses the second fit.

The environment

DFO's model estimates how many cod died of natural causes each year. In the early 1990s that rate jumped far above its 1980s level. The assessment links cod productivity to Capelin, their main prey, and to ocean conditions2. With the environment switched on, this model removes the extra deaths above the 1983–1990 average in each year. This is a proxy. Deaths the assessment cannot explain, possibly including unrecorded catch, also end up in this term. For comparison, the assessment put natural mortality in 2020 at 0.512.

The fleet

In "Write the rules" the fleet follows Gordon–Schaefer logic89: boats enter while fishing is profitable and leave when it isn't. The economic settings below are teaching assumptions. They were not estimated from Newfoundland fleet data. The community regime draws on Ostrom's design principles for self-governed commons10: clear boundaries on who may fish, limits the members set themselves, monitoring by the members, and a cost for doing it.

What it can't tell you

  • It is an illustrative teaching model, not a stock assessment. It has one stock, one kind of boat and no ages, so it can't represent discarding, the collapse of the spawning stock itself, or scientific uncertainty.
  • The assessment series starts in 19832. The model's 1960s stock is set only by the requirement that it could sustain the recorded 1960s catches. For scale, a different, older measure (spawning biomass) is reported as 1.6 million tonnes5 in 1962, falling about 93%5 to 72,000–110,000 tonnes5 by 1992. That is a different quantity from the model line, so it is not plotted.
  • Recorded catch leaves out unreported catch and discards. After the last assessment year the environment term is switched off, which assumes average conditions.
  • The most recent full assessment used here put spawning biomass in 2021 at 411 kt2, 52%2 of the limit reference point. Later assessments revised both the series and the reference point.
